Endpoint, denominators and interpretation

Count one additional person only when a new, eligible breath finding is independently confirmed, for example with an orthogonal assay under a pre-agreed adjudication rule. An adequate standard test with no target, a technical failure, and missing testing are different reasons for no standard result. Analyse those groups separately before comparing sites. Fix the standard pathway and cutoff before reviewing breath results.

The primary denominator includes every eligible participant, including failed or unattempted breath tests. The conditional percentage uses only people without a standard-pathway target. A zero denominator is undefined. The 95% Wilson interval assumes independent participants. Selection and incomplete verification can still bias inference. Clinical benefit, sensitivity and specificity require other endpoints and reference data.

Feasibility · 12 people

Paired DNA sequencing

Tissue and exhaled condensate shared some driver variants in pretreatment lung adenocarcinoma. A small feasibility cohort can show detection but cannot establish population-level added yield.

Recovery · 22 enrolled

A negative experiment

The PLUREX investigators could not recover DNA from breath condensate in their protocol for advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC. Collection and extraction success belong in the denominator.

RNA · Fusion detection

A discordant candidate

A fusion study found SQSTM1–ALK in breath alone. A discordant finding prompts confirmation. Calling it an additional actionable result requires an explicit adjudication rule.
